## KQ2. US government EO procurement — growing or cut in FY2026–FY2027 (NRO EOCL, NGA Luno, Space Force commercial)?

### Takeaway
The FY2026 request proposed ~30% (~$130M) cuts to NRO's EOCL and zeroed a commercial SAR program; Congress moved to restore funding. For FY2027 I found no public dollar line for EOCL, but the Senate Intelligence Committee's Aug 2026 report on the FY27 IAA flagged EOCL flexibility/NRO–NGA coordination concerns, and the Space Force's dedicated commercial-services line was slashed to $23M in the FY27 request (from ~$168M enacted FY26), with appropriators pushing back. Net: US demand is flat-to-uncertain, shifting from imagery buys toward AI analytics (Luno), while international sovereign demand is the growth engine.

## KQ5. Competitive threats — SpaceX/Starshield, Vantor (Maxar), Chinese EO constellations; price pressure

### Takeaway
The NRO's SpaceX-built proliferated constellation (>200 satellites, launches through 2029) is the structural threat to US-government imagery demand, though neither NRO nor SpaceX confirms it is "Starshield." Chinese operators (Chang Guang Jilin-1, SuperView) undercut Western per-km² pricing by ~40–50% ($11.50–14/km² for 50 cm vs $20–25+ Western), and launch-cost deflation lets new entrants and sovereign buyers build their own fleets. Western listed EO names are responding by moving up-stack into AI analytics and sovereign CaaS rather than competing on imagery price.
